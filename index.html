How the model counts
  • One tick is one bus cycle. The device needs the chosen number of ticks to prepare each word.
  • Programmed I/O: the CPU polls the status register through every wait tick, then spends 2 ticks reading and storing the word.
  • Interrupt-driven I/O: the CPU runs other work while the device prepares a word. Each word then costs 4 CPU ticks: 2 to save and restore context, 2 to read and store.
  • DMA, both modes: the CPU spends 2 ticks writing the address, count and control registers, and 1 tick on a completion routine after the interrupt.
  • Cycle stealing: the CPU keeps running during the device wait. DMA then takes 1 bus cycle per word, and the CPU is stalled for that cycle.
  • Burst: 1 tick for HRQ, 1 tick for HLDA, then 1 tick per word with the CPU stalled throughout. It assumes a fast device that holds the whole block.
  • These costs are small round numbers chosen for clarity. Real costs depend on the processor, the bus and the device.
